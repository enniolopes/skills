# Creative direction — find, show and prove the governing idea

Translate strategy into an expressive idea that can generate many coherent decisions. Do not force the first plausible direction to explain itself into permanence, and do not commit a direction nobody has seen.

## 1. Start from decision-changing material

Use the brand job, offer truth, desired meaning, right to win, relevant history/equity, audience context and real constraints as raw material.

References can widen the search space, but decompose them into properties rather than copying their surface. Useful properties may include proportion, rhythm, density, framing, typographic behavior, materiality, motion, verbal behavior or another transferable characteristic.

## 2. Name the floor and the bar

Before committing, name two reference points.

The **floor** is the most available competent-but-generic answer to this brief or category. Ask what a capable designer would produce from category autocomplete alone, which parts of a proposed direction would survive if the company name were swapped for a competitor's, and what in this brief earns any familiar device in use. Do not invert the default mechanically; familiar forms remain valid when causally justified.

The **bar** is two or three real identities from adjacent categories whose level of craft this work must hold beside. Decompose what makes them strong (a typographic decision, a color relationship, a way of using imagery, a compositional habit) so the comparison is about mechanism, not taste. Boards are judged against the bar, not against the brief alone. Correct-and-competent is the floor, and the floor is a failure state.

## 3. Diverge by lineage, not cosmetics

A route is materially different only when its governing idea or generative grammar changes. Font, color, logo-shape or layout swaps inside the same idea are variations, not new routes.

Exploration may be intuitive. Try metaphors, behaviors, tensions, histories, materials, product mechanics, verbal moves, composition logics or other relevant sources without requiring every sketch to carry a full rationale.

Keep exploration disposable. Do not persist rejected routes in canonical state.

## 4. End exploration rendered

Each lineage that survives internal critique becomes a board: a composed visual argument, not a list of adjectives. A board shows the signature at full force in one hero moment on a declared touchpoint, the typographic voice on real words, the color relationship at scale, and one dense application next to one expressive one. Generated imagery may be used here as exploration, whether or not final assets will be generated; load `visual-production.md` when it is.

Make the boards comparable: same touchpoints, same content, different ideas. Judge what the boards actually produce. A direction can discover a stronger framing than the initial verbal strategy; it may refine creative interpretation, but it may not invent business truth.

## 5. Find a thesis with generative force

A useful creative thesis is:
- narrower than the whole strategy;
- richer than a mood adjective;
- specific enough to affect real choices;
- broad enough to work across more than one medium;
- capable of producing variation without losing family resemblance.

Avoid empty destinations such as `modern`, `premium`, `innovative`, `trustworthy`, `bold` or `human` unless the thesis explains what behavior makes the quality perceptible.

## 6. Present, then earn commitment

The owner sees the boards ranked, with the recommendation and the reasons, and with the names the system's elements will carry. The owner ratifies or redirects; the agent does not proceed to build on an unseen direction. Present a recommendation, not an unranked menu.

Commit a working direction when it demonstrates:
- clear dependence on this brand;
- strategic relevance;
- distinctiveness without gratuitous novelty;
- craft that holds beside the named bar;
- ability to generate a system rather than one hero image;
- compatibility with existing equity that still matters;
- enough range to survive the declared touchpoints;
- plausible production feasibility.

At commitment time, make the rationale explicit enough that future work can preserve the idea without copying an execution. The durable direction compresses to **thesis**, **principles**, **signature** and, when needed, **excludes**.

## 7. Know when to abandon the direction

Re-diverge when representative use shows that the thesis:
- produces generic/category-coded work;
- needs constant exceptions;
- cannot generate enough range;
- relies on polish, novelty or symbolism to seem strong;
- conflicts with real content/touchpoints;
- cannot explain recurring system failures.

Refine instead when the thesis remains strong and the defect is downstream craft or execution.

## Negative control

Do not use:
- symbolism as a substitute for meaning;
- novelty or complexity as a substitute for sophistication;
- style signals as a substitute for specificity or premium quality;
- repetition as a substitute for coherence;
- a polished presentation as a substitute for a working system;
- a written rationale as a substitute for a board someone has looked at.

The mechanism remains valid when the brief independently earns it.
