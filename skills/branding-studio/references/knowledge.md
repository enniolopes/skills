# Knowledge calibration

Match claim strength to evidence. Do not flatten useful branding ideas into “consensus” or present heuristics as laws.

## ROBUST PRINCIPLE

Treat these as broad operating principles supported by repeated professional convergence and/or strong reasoning:
- strategy/research should precede irreversible identity decisions;
- identity is broader than a logo; verbal, visual and experiential cues operate as a system;
- repetition and consistency help assets accumulate recognition; unnecessary change can destroy equity;
- trial applications expose system failures that isolated logo boards cannot;
- brand perception/reputation exists in people, not merely in organizational declarations;
- accessibility, legibility, production feasibility and legal constraints affect real identity performance.

## ESTABLISHED PRACTICE

Use when appropriate, not as universal law:
- competitive/category audits;
- positioning platforms and creative briefs;
- naming pipelines with linguistic/legal triage;
- voice principles and tone-by-context;
- grids, type systems, clear-space rules and monochrome/reduced variants;
- brand guidelines/centers and asset management;
- realistic application testing;
- versioning and migration during rebrands.

## EMPIRICAL FINDING

Use directionally and preserve scope/sample limits:
- distinctive-asset research indicates asset types differ in average fame/uniqueness; shape/logo often performs more strongly than color in reported samples. Do not turn sample averages into universal portfolio-distance weights;
- mental availability and category-entry-point concepts have strong evidence in mature consumer-brand research; transfer to small-base deep-tech/institutional contexts requires judgment;
- sound symbolism/phonosemantics has replicated but modest, context-dependent effects;
- OKLab/OKLCH are useful perceptual color spaces for technical manipulation/comparison.

## CONTESTED / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

Use only with an explicit caveat:
- brand archetypes as personality/storytelling heuristics;
- universal color psychology such as “blue means trust”;
- category-design claims about dominant “category kings”;
- fixed formulas for concept count, font count, color count or grid units.

## LEGAL / CURRENT FACT

Verify against current authoritative sources when material:
- INPI procedures, fees, timelines and filing rules;
- Nice-class interpretation for a specific filing;
- trademark conflicts/registrability;
- domain/handle availability;
- accessibility/legal obligations in a jurisdiction.

Triage is not definitive legal advice. Require qualified counsel when stakes justify it.

### WCAG / APCA

- WCAG 2.x contrast criteria are a widely used accessibility-conformance baseline for web/digital content.
- Do not call WCAG a universal legal floor; applicable law varies by jurisdiction/context.
- Treat APCA/Lc as a perceptual contrast quality signal associated with evolving accessibility-standard work, not an adopted universal compliance requirement.

## MYTH / NON-EVIDENCE

Do not use as proof:
- golden ratio as a universal reason a logo is good;
- “a color means X emotion” (“blue = trust”) as sufficient rationale;
- brand archetypes, fixed font counts, modular scales or 4/8pt grids as universal rules rather than optional tools;
- “minimal geometric sans = modern/professional” as neutral best practice;
- precise numerical brand/creative scores without validated measurement;
- a generated image being unique merely because it looks original;
- model agreement or self-critique as market validation;
- structural JSON validity proving strategic or creative quality.

## CONTEXT NOTES

### Research institute / ICT
Watch for:
- scientific/institutional authority;
- funders, government, academia and industry as distinct stakeholders;
- long lifespan;
- documentation/formal applications;
- multilingual/acronym considerations;
- architecture with university/lab/parent institution.

Do not equate sobriety with generic visual conservatism.

### Deep-tech B2B startup
Watch for:
- alternatives such as internal process, spreadsheets and status quo;
- technical buyer plus executive/investor audiences;
- product/category movement;
- naming scope tolerance;
- credibility under technical scrutiny without category blanding.

## Boundaries

- **Real perception/fame/uniqueness** requires field evidence.
- **Definitive trademark clearance** requires appropriate legal review.
- **Production master** requires reproducible craft/tooling; concept authority is broader than production authority.
- **Aesthetic preference** may decide among strategically valid routes, but must not be disguised as compliance or evidence.
