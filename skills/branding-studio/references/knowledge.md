# Knowledge calibration

Match claim strength to evidence. Branding frameworks are tools, not laws; professional convention must not impersonate empirical proof.

## Contested or weak as universal rules

Do not present as law:
- brand archetypes;
- universal color psychology (`blue = trust`, etc.);
- golden-ratio explanations of logo quality;
- fixed formulas for concept count, font count, color count or grid units;
- "minimal geometric sans = modern/professional", "serif = premium/trustworthy";
- novelty, complexity or unusual layout as proof of creativity or sophistication;
- precise numerical creative-quality scores without validated measurement;
- sound symbolism/phonosemantics beyond a modest secondary signal that never overrides meaning, language reality or legal risk.

A distinctive asset is one that is actually recognized; do not assume a cue is distinctive because it was intentionally designed that way. Mental-availability and category-entry-point frameworks are useful where the market and research model fit, not mandatory strategy fields.

## Current and legal facts

Verify current authoritative sources when materially relevant: trademark procedures, fees, timelines and filing rules; classification for a specific filing; current domain/handle availability; jurisdiction-specific accessibility or legal obligations; current competitor and product facts.

Trademark searching by the agent is triage, not definitive legal clearance. Escalate to qualified counsel when consequence justifies it.

## Accessibility

WCAG 2.x contrast criteria are a widely used digital baseline, not a universal legal floor; applicable law varies by jurisdiction and product context. APCA/Lc is a useful additional perceptual signal, not universally adopted compliance.
